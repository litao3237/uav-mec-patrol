# English captions for Fig. 3–13 and Fig. S1–S2

## Shared reporting conventions

Unless otherwise stated, each setting contains three scenario seeds (45–47) crossed with three algorithm seeds (100–102), using 100 ALNS iterations and two elite rounds. These nine runs provide descriptive statistics across the specified scenario/seed combinations; they are not nine independent geographic scenarios. Main energy is total UAV energy from strictly optimal Stage-1 resource solves, displayed in kJ. Stage-2 QoS and resource-utilization summaries include only strictly optimal Stage-2 results. Conditional means may refer to different subsets at different settings. Missing outcomes are not replaced by zero. No significance tests or significance markers are implied. Greedy denotes Greedy + MEC Repair; FR-NM denotes Fixed-Route Nearest-MEC; Route-GA denotes Route-GA + deterministic MEC repair.

**Fig. 3. Energy and paired improvement across task workloads.** (a) Mean strictly evaluated UAV energy of Generic ALNS and Proposed Hybrid for K = 30–80, with M = 5 UAVs and E = 2 MEC servers. Error bars show one sample standard deviation across the nine runs at each K (ddof = 1). Both methods are Stage-1 strict in all nine runs at every workload. (b) Mean paired energy improvement, computed as the mean of 100 × (E_Generic − E_Hybrid)/E_Generic over matching scenario/algorithm seeds. Blue circles and dashed lines denote Generic ALNS; orange squares and solid lines denote Proposed Hybrid. Lines connect tested workloads for readability.

**Fig. 4. Evolution of the Hybrid solution structure with workload.** Mean (a) fraction of offloaded tasks, (b) contact visits per UAV, and (c) total route distance across all UAVs for K = 30–80, M = 5, and E = 2. Each point uses nine Stage-1-strict Hybrid solutions. These are discrete solution-structure summaries; they do not use the smaller Stage-2 QoS subset. Offloading is shown as a percentage of tasks, while contacts are normalized by the number of UAVs.

**Fig. 5. Baseline energy quality and high-load strict feasibility.** Both panels include the four baselines (Greedy + MEC Repair, FR-NM, Route-GA, and Generic ALNS) and Proposed Hybrid, for five methods in total. (a) Individual Stage-1-strict energies at K = 50, M = 5, and E = 2, with horizontal marks indicating conditional method means. Labels show strict/scheduled counts: Greedy contributes two strict results from three unique scenarios, FR-NM contributes three from three, and Route-GA, Generic ALNS, and Proposed Hybrid each contribute nine from nine runs. The unsuccessful Greedy scenario contributes no energy point; its mean therefore represents a smaller scenario subset and is not an all-scenario mean. Horizontal offsets separate observations and have no quantitative meaning. (b) Stage-1-strict outcomes at K = 80, with the actual numerator and denominator shown above each bar. Greedy and FR-NM are counted once per scenario. The Route-GA result is a three-scenario high-budget pilot, not a nine-run matrix; search budgets are not matched across methods.

**Fig. 6. Paired energy comparison of Generic ALNS and Proposed Hybrid.** Each row joins the two strictly evaluated energies for one scenario/algorithm-seed combination at (a) K = 50 or (b) K = 80, with M = 5 and E = 2. Open blue circles denote Generic ALNS and orange squares denote Proposed Hybrid. Coincident symbols indicate equal energies at the reporting tolerance. Hybrid improves five of nine pairs at K = 50 and eight of nine pairs at K = 80, with four and one equal pairs, respectively, and no worse pairs. Seed labels identify the pairing; the horizontal axis retains absolute energy.

**Fig. 7. Contribution of elite-refinement operation families.** (a) Mean paired energy advantage of the full Hybrid algorithm over four ablated variants at K = 80, M = 5, and E = 2. The variants remove route–computation relocation, the contact family, explicit batch moves, or progressive widening. Each comparison shares the Generic ALNS exploration result and contains nine common-strict pairs. (b) Counts of pairs in which the full algorithm is better, equal, or worse (the ablated variant is better). The zero advantage for removing progressive widening is shown explicitly. Percentages are paired statistics from the formal ablation summary, not differences of aggregate percentage gains against Generic ALNS.

**Fig. 8. Sensitivity to MEC-server and UAV counts.** The upper row varies E = 2, 3, 4 at K = 100 and M = 5, showing (a) Stage-1-strict rate, (b) offloading fraction, and (c) mean active-MEC CPU utilization. The lower row varies M = 3, 5, 8 at K = 80 and E = 2, showing (d) Stage-1-strict rate, (e) mean task delay, and (f) contacts per UAV. Rate bars report the strict count out of nine scheduled runs. Conditional metrics follow the published Stage-2 subset: n = 3, 5, 5 in the MEC sweep and n = 0, 8, 8 in the UAV sweep. N/A at M = 3 means that no strict terminal solution was recovered within the fixed search budget; it does not establish global infeasibility.

**Fig. 9. Sensitivity to the maximum number of contact visits per UAV.** With K = 80, M = 5, and E = 2, only the per-UAV contact limit is varied from one to four; task attributes, geometry, and seed combinations remain fixed. Panels show (a) Stage-1-strict rate, (b) conditional mean UAV energy, and (c) realized contact visits per UAV. Energy and contact means use the Stage-1-strict subsets, with n = 6, 7, 9, 9 explicitly shown. The slightly higher mean energy at a limit of four reflects outcomes of finite-budget heuristic search and does not imply that additional contact opportunities intrinsically worsen the optimum.

**Fig. 10. Bandwidth sensitivity and marginal resource values.** (a) Conditional mean Stage-1 UAV energy at bandwidth multipliers 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5, with K = 80, M = 5, and E = 2; strict sample counts are n = 8, 9, 9. (b) Mean relative shadow prices of bandwidth and MEC CPU capacity on the same Stage-1-strict subsets. A relative shadow price is the Stage-1 capacity multiplier multiplied by the corresponding resource capacity and divided by the Stage-1 optimal energy. It describes a local marginal value for the fixed discrete structure, not Stage-2 utilization. Values displayed as 0.000 are rounded at the precision of the published CSV and should not be interpreted as exact analytical zeros. Hatching distinguishes CPU bars in grayscale.

**Fig. 11. Iteration-budget sensitivity of Proposed Hybrid.** (a) Conditional mean Stage-1 energy and (b) mean runtime for independently executed budgets of 25, 50, 100, and 200 iterations at K = 80, M = 5, and E = 2. Labels in panel (a) report strict counts of 6/9, 8/9, 9/9, and 7/9. Runtime averages include all nine scheduled runs at each budget. The same scenario/algorithm-seed combinations are used, but the acceptance schedule depends on the total budget, so these points are not prefixes of one convergence trajectory. Conditional energy means refer to different strict subsets.

**Fig. 12. Standard-search quality relative to an empirical strong reference.** At K = 28, M = 2, and E = 2, (a) each point shows the standard Hybrid run's gap, 100 × (E_standard − E_best-known)/E_best-known, for one algorithm seed within the indicated scenario. Horizontal marks are scenario means. The standard setting uses seeds 100–102, 100 iterations, and two elite rounds. Strong search uses seeds 700–711, 1000 iterations, six elite rounds, and enlarged elite candidate budgets. The best-known energy is the minimum strictly evaluated energy observed for that scenario in the formal reference experiment. (b) Mean runtime over nine standard runs and 36 strong runs. The reference is empirical best-known and supplies no global-optimality certificate.

**Fig. 13. GIS-driven real-geography case study in the Stanislaus area.** (a) Five Hybrid UAV routes over 59 fixed USFS historical fire-occurrence locations, with a depot at Groveland and two modeled MEC sites. Coordinates are displayed in UTM zone 10N (EPSG:32610). Line styles distinguish UAVs; open circles mark task sites, the square marks the depot, triangles mark MEC sites, the diamond marks the contact, and the star marks the offloaded task. Dashed coverage boundaries show the modeled MEC radii (600 m at E1 and 800 m at E2); the outer dotted circle marks the 4 km study area. The U5 detail shows the contact at E1, co-located with the depot, and offloaded task S40. Route segments are flight paths; collected data are carried to the contact. The displayed S45/A100 solution was reproduced using the formal source and parameters (100 iterations, two elite rounds), yielding strict Stage-1 energy 869,675.432 J, total distance 47,645.919 m, one contact, and one offloaded task. (b) Strict feasibility uses three unique scenarios for deterministic baselines and nine runs for each ALNS method. (c) Eight pairs in which both methods are Stage-1 strict; Hybrid improves two and ties six, with mean paired improvement 0.543%. Basemap: USGS The National Map; task locations: USFS historical fire records. This is a GIS-driven computational case study with modeled MEC deployments, not a field-flight experiment or a claim that sensors were installed at historical fire sites.

**Fig. S1. Robustness to task-site spatial distributions.** For uniform, clustered, and boundary-biased task sites at K = 80, M = 5, and E = 2, panels show (a) Stage-1-strict rate, (b) conditional mean offloading fraction, and (c) mean Stage-1 relative bandwidth shadow price. Task data-size and processing-demand random streams are held fixed while spatial distributions change. Conditional metrics use the indicated strict subsets of n = 9, 7, 6. Absolute energies are not compared here because the spatial distributions also change route geometry. The shadow-price definition is the same as in Fig. 10.

**Fig. S2. Sensitivity to MEC coverage radius.** Conditional mean (a) Stage-1 UAV energy and (b) total route distance for coverage-radius multipliers 0.75, 1.0, and 1.25 at K = 80, M = 5, and E = 2. All settings have nine strict runs, shown at each point. Changing coverage radius changes candidate-contact geometry, communication distances, and the structures found under the fixed search budget. The observed nonmonotonic response should therefore be read as contact-geometry sensitivity rather than a monotonic capacity guarantee.

## Data provenance and presentation

All curves and bars use the unchanged `paper_results/*.csv` summaries. Run-level scatter, pairs, and Fig. 3 sample standard deviations use cached official GitHub artifacts, re-aggregated and checked against CSV rounding precision. Sensitivity and ablation tables provide aggregate summaries only; no artificial run-level distributions or error bars were synthesized. Formal artifact/run/commit identifiers and SHA-256 hashes are in `data/experiment_sources.json`; preserved-original hashes are in `data/preserved_files.json`. The route cache records the replay environment and exact comparison with the formal run. Normal figure rebuilding uses these local snapshots without network access.
